1. Island Travels gives us land, shallow water, and blocked deep water. We may start on any island and want the least swimming needed to visit them all.

2. First, flood-fill connected X cells. Each four-directionally connected group receives one island number, and there are at most fifteen groups.

3. Next, turn the grid into a smaller graph. Every island becomes a vertex, and an edge records the cheapest number of shallow squares between two islands.

4. We find those edge costs with zero-one BFS. Moving onto island land costs zero, moving onto shallow water costs one, and deep water cannot be entered.

5. Start each search from every cell of one island at distance zero. Zero-cost moves go to the front of the deque, while one-cost moves go to the back.

6. After one search, the smallest distance on each target island is its pairwise travel cost. Repeat this for every source island.

7. Now solve the visit order with bitmask DP. A mask says which islands are visited, and last says where the route currently ends.

8. Every one-island state starts at zero because the helicopter may land on any first island. Add an unvisited island using its pairwise swimming cost.

9. Zero-one BFS guarantees every graph edge is realizable and cheapest. The subset DP checks every possible island order, so their combination finds the global optimum.

10. The expensive part is roughly K squared times two to the K, with K at most fifteen. Random maps and a fifteen-island chain verify all three stages.
