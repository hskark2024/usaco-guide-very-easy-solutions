1. Meet in the Middle asks us to count subsets that add to a target. With forty numbers, checking all two-to-the-forty subsets is far too much work.

2. The trick is to split the array into two halves. Each half has at most twenty numbers, so it has about one million subsets instead of one trillion.

3. For each half, create a list of every subset sum. Start with zero for the empty subset. When reading a new value, copy every old sum and add the value to each copy.

4. Now every full subset is a pair: one subset chosen from the left and one chosen from the right. Their two sums must add to the target.

5. Sort the right-half sums. For a left sum s, the partner we need is exactly target minus s.

6. Use lower bound and upper bound to find the whole block of matching right sums. The block length tells us how many right subsets work with this left subset.

7. Keep duplicates. If two different right subsets both total three, they are two different choices and both must count.

8. In the sample, values one, two, three, two target five. The valid choices are two different one-two-two style index choices and the two-plus-three choice, for three total subsets.

9. Every original subset splits in exactly one way at the middle, so it is counted once. Every pair we count adds exactly to the target, so it is valid.

10. The running time is roughly N times two to the N over two. Random brute force and the forty-ones stress case verify both multiplicity and 64-bit counting.
