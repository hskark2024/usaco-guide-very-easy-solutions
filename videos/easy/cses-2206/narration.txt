1. Pizzeria Queries puts a pizzeria and an apartment in every building on one street. The delivered cost is the menu price plus the distance between buildings.

2. We process price changes and questions asking for the cheapest delivered pizza at one building. Scanning all pizzerias for every question is too slow.

3. The absolute value is the obstacle, so split possible sources into two sides of the customer.

4. If pizzeria i is left of customer k, the cost is price i plus k minus i. Rewrite that as price i minus i, plus k.

5. If pizzeria i is right of k, the cost is price i plus i minus k. Rewrite that as price i plus i, minus k.

6. Now every building contributes two transformed numbers: price minus index and price plus index. A segment-tree node stores the minimum of both values.

7. For a customer at k, query the minimum price-minus-index on the prefix ending at k and add k.

8. Query the minimum price-plus-index on the suffix starting at k and subtract k. The smaller candidate is the overall answer.

9. When a pizzeria changes its price, update both transformed values at its leaf and repair the ancestors.

10. Each update or question takes logarithmic time. Random tests compare the tree with a direct scan of every pizzeria, including customers at both ends of the street.
