1. Xor-Paths asks us to move right or down across a grid and count the routes whose cell values XOR to one target.

2. A twenty by twenty grid has over thirty-five billion full paths, but each half of a path has at most nineteen moves. That suggests meet in the middle.

3. Stop every forward route after half the moves. They all land on one anti-diagonal. For each meeting cell, count how often each partial XOR appears.

4. Now start at the finish and enumerate the other half backward, moving only up or left. Each backward route reaches the same anti-diagonal.

5. There is one XOR detail to handle. Both partial routes include the meeting cell, so combining their XOR values cancels that cell because equal values XOR to zero.

6. To include the meeting value exactly once, the full XOR is forward XOR backward XOR meeting value. Rearranging gives the forward value we need.

7. We look up target XOR backward XOR meeting value in that cell's forward frequency map. The stored frequency is the number of full routes contributed.

8. Every complete route has exactly one meeting cell and one split into these two halves, so no route is missed or counted twice.

9. A one-cell grid also works without special code: both searches stop immediately, and the duplicated meeting-cell correction tests its value against the target.

10. The work is about two to the power of half the path length instead of enumerating every path. Exhaustive small grids and a twenty by twenty stress case verify the implementation.
