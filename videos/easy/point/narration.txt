You are standing at point P one and looking toward point P two. Is point P three on your left, on your right, or directly on the same line? A signed cross product answers all three cases with one exact calculation.

First form two arrows that both start at P one. The first arrow goes to P two, and the second goes to P three. If their coordinates are D X one, D Y one and D X two, D Y two, compute D X one times D Y two minus D Y one times D X two.

This number is the signed area of the parallelogram made by the arrows. A positive area means turning counterclockwise from the line arrow to the point arrow, so P three is on the left. A negative area means a clockwise turn, so it is on the right. Zero area means the arrows are parallel, placing all three points on the same line, which the problem calls touch.

For the sample line from one, one to five, three, the point two, three gives a positive cross product, so it is left. Point four, one gives a negative value, so it is right. Point three, two gives zero and touches the line.

The coordinate limits make ordinary thirty-two-bit integers unsafe. The solution uses one-hundred-twenty-eight-bit integer arithmetic, so even extreme products and their subtraction remain exact. Each test uses constant time and constant memory, making one hundred thousand tests easy to handle.
