1. Secret gives us a fixed array and a black-box function. We must combine every value in a requested range from left to right.

2. The function is associative, so parentheses may move. But it might not be commutative, so we must never change the order of the values.

3. Split the array at its midpoint. For every position on the left, precompute the ordered combination from that position through the midpoint.

4. For every position on the right, precompute the ordered combination from just after the midpoint through that position.

5. Then recurse inside both halves. Each depth prepares crossing answers for smaller and smaller segments.

6. During a query, descend until the two endpoints first fall on different sides of a split. That split has exactly the two pieces we need.

7. The saved left suffix comes first, and the saved right prefix comes second. One Secret call joins them into the whole answer.

8. A one-element query simply returns its original value and needs zero black-box calls. In the sample, the answers are thirteen, thirty-two, eight, and thirteen.

9. Associativity proves the two prepared pieces may be joined. Their element order is unchanged, so this remains correct even when swapping arguments would fail.

10. Preprocessing takes O of N log N calls and memory. Each query uses logarithmic comparisons but at most one Secret call, verified by random left-to-right folds.
