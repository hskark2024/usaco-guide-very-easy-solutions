Imagine a ball moving through a row of holes. Hole i sends it forward by its power. We need fast updates and fast questions about where the ball leaves and how many jumps it makes.

Following every jump is too slow when every power is one. One question could cross all one hundred thousand holes.

Split the row into blocks of about square root of N holes. For each starting hole, save a shortcut for the entire part of its path inside the current block.

That shortcut stores three facts: the first destination outside the block, the number of jumps used to get there, and the last hole visited before leaving the block.

We build a block from right to left. Every power is positive, so a jump that stays in the block lands at a position whose shortcut is already known. We add one jump and copy the rest of that shortcut.

If the jump leaves the block immediately, the destination is already our next position, the jump count is one, and the current hole is the last visited hole in this block.

To answer a question, begin at the requested hole and consume one shortcut at a time. Add its jump count and remember its last visited hole. After only about square root of N block transitions, the next destination is outside the row.

When one power changes, only its own block needs rebuilding. Earlier blocks stop their summaries at the boundary, so they do not store details from this changed block.

The right-to-left construction is correct by induction. The rightmost hole has a direct summary. Each earlier hole either leaves the block or joins a summary we already proved correct.

Test a long chain of power one, jumps across block boundaries, immediate exits, and updates on the edges of blocks. Both an update and a question take about square root of N time.
