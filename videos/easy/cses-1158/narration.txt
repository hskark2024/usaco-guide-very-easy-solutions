Hey everyone. Today we are solving Book Shop from CSES and the current USACO Guide Easy list. This problem is a clean introduction to zero-one knapsack.

There are N books. Each book has a price and a page count. We have a budget X, and we want as many total pages as possible. Every physical book can be bought at most once.

A greedy rule such as most pages, cheapest price, or best pages per dollar can fail. A slightly worse-looking book may combine with another book to fit the budget perfectly. We need dynamic programming to compare all useful combinations without listing every subset.

Define best of money as the maximum number of pages we can obtain using only the books processed so far while spending at most that amount. Initially every entry is zero because buying nothing is allowed for any budget.

Suppose the current book costs p and contains v pages. For every capacity money, we have two choices. Skip the book and keep best of money. Or buy it, leaving money minus p for earlier books, and earn best of money minus p plus v. We store the larger result.

We compress the usual two-dimensional table into one row. That saves memory, but it makes loop direction critical. Scan money from X down to p. The source best of money minus p still belongs to the previous-book state. If we scan upward, a state updated with this book could feed another state in the same pass, letting us buy the same physical book several times.

Trace the official sample. Prices are four, eight, five, and three. Pages are five, twelve, eight, and one. The budget is ten. The book costing eight gives twelve pages. The books costing four and five fit together for nine dollars and give thirteen pages. No other legal set gives more, so the answer is thirteen.

Notice that best of money means spending at most money. We do not need an impossible marker because the empty selection is legal at every capacity. The table is also monotonic in the sense that extra budget never hurts, although the transition does not need to enforce that separately.

For correctness, use induction on processed books. Before any books, zero pages is optimal. For a new book, every valid selection either excludes it or includes it exactly once. Excluding it is represented by the existing state. Including it leaves a selection made only from earlier books that fits in the remaining budget. The transition takes the better of these complete and disjoint cases. The downward loop prevents the current book from appearing in its own source state.

The C plus plus code reads the price list and page list, creates X plus one integers, then performs one descending pass per book. The running time is O of N times X, and the memory is O of X.

Check an edge case with one book costing six and a budget of five. Every table entry stays zero. Now give the same book a budget of six: the update reaches capacity six exactly and stores its pages.

Duplicate prices are fine. The algorithm loops over book indices, so two books with the same cost remain separate choices. It may buy either one or both when the budget allows.
