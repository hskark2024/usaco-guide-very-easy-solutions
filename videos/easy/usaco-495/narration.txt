1. Marathon describes a route through checkpoints on a city grid. A runner follows them in order, but may skip one checkpoint inside a requested sub-route.

2. We also move checkpoints between questions. Recomputing every route and trying every skip would be far too slow.

3. First ignore the skip. The route distance is simply the sum of Manhattan distances between neighboring checkpoints.

4. Skipping checkpoint i replaces the edge into i and the edge out of i with one direct edge from its previous checkpoint to its next checkpoint.

5. The saving is the two old edge lengths minus that direct distance. Because Manhattan distance obeys the triangle inequality, this saving is never negative.

6. For a query from l to r, find the sum of all adjacent edges in that interval. Then find the largest saving centered strictly between l and r.

7. Subtracting that best saving gives the shortest allowed route. If there is no interior checkpoint, the saving is zero.

8. We maintain edge lengths in a sum segment tree and skip savings in a maximum segment tree. Both range results take logarithmic time.

9. Moving one checkpoint changes only its edge to the left, its edge to the right, and the three skip triples that contain it. Everything farther away stays valid.

10. Random tests directly rebuild each small queried path and try every legal skip. The official example and a full-size update stream check both correctness and performance.
