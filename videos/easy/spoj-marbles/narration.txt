1. MARBLES asks how many ways identical marbles can be split among several colors, with every color receiving at least one marble.

2. Positive groups are slightly awkward. Fix that first by handing one marble to every color. Now the remaining marbles may be distributed with zero allowed.

3. Represent each remaining marble by a star. Put separators between stars to create one group for each color.

4. With N total marbles and K colors, this picture contains N minus K stars and K minus one separators, so it has N minus one positions altogether.

5. Choosing the separator positions gives N minus one choose K minus one. This is the stars-and-bars formula for positive group sizes.

6. To calculate the binomial coefficient, use its smaller side. N choose K equals N choose N minus K, so fewer loop steps are always better.

7. Start at one. At step i, multiply by the next numerator factor and divide by i. Every division is exact because each partial value is another binomial coefficient.

8. The final answer fits in a signed 64-bit integer, but multiplying before dividing can briefly be larger. A 128-bit temporary keeps that intermediate safe.

9. For thirty marbles and seven colors, the answer is twenty-nine choose six, which equals four hundred seventy-five thousand twenty.

10. The proof is a one-to-one translation between distributions and star-separator strings. Exact integer tests cover one color, one marble per color, symmetry, random cases, and large inputs.
