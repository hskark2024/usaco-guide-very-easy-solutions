1. Robot Collisions has robots moving between two reflecting walls. We need the explosion time of every robot, but only meetings at integer coordinates count.

2. Start with parity. At any integer time, a robot keeps the parity of its starting coordinate plus the time. Two different starting parities can never share an integer coordinate.

3. So we split the robots into even and odd groups. Inside each group, sort by position while remembering every robot's original input index.

4. During a left-to-right scan, a right-moving robot waits on a stack. When a left-moving robot appears, it pairs with the nearest waiting right-mover.

5. That direct pair meets after half their distance. No outside robot can reach either one sooner, so removing the pair is safe.

6. Any left-mover with no waiting right-mover must first bounce off wall zero. After direct pairs disappear, these leftover left-movers are all before the leftover right-movers.

7. Pair leftover left-movers from left to right. Unfolding the reflection shows that their meeting time is their two starting positions added together, divided by two.

8. Pair leftover right-movers from right to left. Their time is two M minus both positions, divided by two.

9. If one left-mover and one right-mover remain, both use a wall. Their unfolded meeting time is two M plus the left position minus the right position, all divided by two.

10. A single remaining robot has no same-parity partner, so its answer stays minus one. The four pairing cases cover every possible collision.

11. Sorting costs N log N and the scans are linear. A separate time-step simulator checks random small corridors, reflections, and multi-robot meetings.
