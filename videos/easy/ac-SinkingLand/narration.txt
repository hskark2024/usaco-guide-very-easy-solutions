1. Sinking Land is an elevation grid surrounded by sea. Each year the water rises, but a low cell sinks only after water can reach it from the outside.

2. Every cell has two requirements. It must touch the sea, directly or through sunk neighbors, and the current year must reach its elevation.

3. Suppose a cell first becomes sea-adjacent in year t. Its earliest sinking year is the larger of t and its elevation.

4. This formula suggests buckets. Bucket y stores every cell whose earliest sinking time is exactly year y.

5. Boundary cells already touch the outside sea at year zero, so we schedule each boundary cell at its elevation. A visited flag prevents duplicate corners.

6. Now process years in increasing order. Every cell in the current bucket sinks, so we subtract one from the remaining land area.

7. When a cell sinks, each unseen neighbor becomes sea-adjacent for the first time. We schedule that neighbor at the larger of the current year and its own elevation.

8. A low neighbor may join the same year's bucket. The processing loop keeps going through newly appended cells, which captures the whole instant chain reaction.

9. Each cell is scheduled at most once. Cells whose sinking year is beyond the requested horizon need no bucket because they cannot change an answer.

10. The total time is H times W plus Y, with linear memory. A separate flood fill recomputed from the boundary checks hundreds of random small grids.
