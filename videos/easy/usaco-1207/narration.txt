1. Robot Instructions gives us movement cards. We need to reach one goal point and report how many winning subsets use exactly K cards for every K.

2. Forty cards create too many full subsets, so split them into left and right halves with at most twenty cards each.

3. Every half-subset has three useful facts: its total x movement, its total y movement, and how many cards it used.

4. Enumerate all right subsets. Group them by cards used, and inside each group count how many subsets reach each coordinate.

5. Now enumerate a left subset that reaches point p using i cards. The right half must supply exactly goal minus p.

6. Look up that needed point in every right-size group j. If its frequency is f, add f to answer bucket i plus j.

7. Frequencies matter because duplicate movement cards can create the same coordinate in several different ways. Each index subset is a separate answer.

8. Negative moves work naturally. Subtracting the left point from the goal finds the correct complement whether movement goes right, left, up, or down.

9. Each winning full subset has exactly one left part and one right part. Our lookup finds it once and places it in the bucket for its total number of cards.

10. This takes roughly N times two to the N over two expected time. Brute-force random grids and a forty-identical-move binomial case verify every output line.
