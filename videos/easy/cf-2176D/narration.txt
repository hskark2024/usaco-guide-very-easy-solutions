1. Fibonacci Paths gives every vertex a positive number. We count directed paths with at least one edge where each value after the first two equals their sum.

2. Any directed edge is already a valid path. A two-number sequence has no recurrence check yet, so the edge by itself contributes one answer.

3. Suppose the first edge is u to v. A third vertex must have value a of u plus a of v. That turns the graph problem into a lookup by value.

4. Define ways at u and x as the number of valid paths starting at u whose second vertex has value x. Different neighbors with the same value share one bucket.

5. For edge u to v, begin with one for the edge alone. Then add the bucket at v keyed by a of u plus a of v. These are exactly its legal continuations.

6. There seems to be a circular dependency because graph edges may form cycles. Positive labels give us the order that breaks this cycle.

7. A continuation edge has a larger endpoint sum than the current edge. Sort every edge by endpoint sum from largest to smallest, and every needed state is ready on time.

8. Add the computed edge count into the bucket at u keyed by a of v. Also add it to the global answer, all modulo nine hundred ninety-eight million two hundred forty-four thousand three hundred fifty-three.

9. Values strictly grow from the third term onward. A counted route cannot return to an earlier vertex value, so the dynamic program automatically counts simple paths.

10. Random small graphs are checked by literally listing every simple path. Large labels and a two-hundred-thousand-edge case test overflow safety and performance.
