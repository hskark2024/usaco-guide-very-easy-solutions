1. In CSES Hamiltonian Flights, we start at city one, finish at city N, and visit every city exactly once. We count all directed routes, so a simple visited array is not enough.

2. N is at most twenty. That is a clue to represent the visited set with a bitmask. Bit i is one exactly when city i has already been visited.

3. Our state is D P of mask and last. It counts routes that start at city one, visit exactly the mask, and currently finish at last.

4. To compute a state, remove last from the mask. Then try every previous city in the smaller mask that has a directed flight into last. Add its route count.

5. Every route has exactly one final flight, so these transitions neither miss nor double-count a route. We take every addition modulo one billion and seven.

6. In the four-city sample, the middle cities can be visited as two then three, or three then two. Both orders have all needed flights, so the answer is two.

7. Two prunings matter. Every mask must include city one, and city N cannot appear before the full mask because it must be the final city.

8. There are N times two-to-the-N states, and each may inspect up to N predecessors. That gives O of N squared times two-to-the-N time and O of N times two-to-the-N space.
