1. The Sports Festival lets us reorder relay runners. After each runner, we pay the fastest speed seen minus the slowest speed seen, and we want the smallest total.

2. Sort all speeds on a number line. The discrepancy of any chosen group depends only on its leftmost and rightmost values.

3. It helps to think backward. The final stage always contains everyone, so its cost is the overall maximum minus the overall minimum.

4. Just before the final stage, an optimal order can be missing one extreme: either the smallest speed or the largest speed.

5. Removing a middle value would leave the same minimum and maximum. Moving that middle runner earlier cannot increase the cost, so an extreme can safely be last.

6. Define DP left right as the smallest cost for the sorted interval from left through right. A one-person interval costs zero.

7. For a longer interval, choose the cheaper smaller state: remove the left endpoint or remove the right endpoint.

8. Then add the current interval width, speed right minus speed left. That width is the discrepancy after all runners in this interval have appeared.

9. For speeds one, two, and three, the best costs grow from zero to one to three. This matches an order beginning near the middle.

10. There are quadratic intervals and constant work per interval, so time and memory are O of N squared. Brute-force permutations verify small arrays.
